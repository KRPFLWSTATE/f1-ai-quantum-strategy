This corrected revision supersedes version 3.0 and retains the redesign of version 2. It
proposes a study; it reports no new experimental results. Implementation, protocol freeze,
physical quantum execution, manuscript writing and journal selection remain subsequent
stages.
Decision. Proceed with a staged, zero additional spending research project. The
strongest defensible direction is an auditable study of when a learned quantum candidate
generator can contribute to a two-car pit decision before a deadline, and when the available
classical solution or model uncertainty leaves it no useful role.
The immediate deliverable is a reproducible motorsport decision benchmark, a learned
parameter-selection component, carefully controlled circuit comparisons, and an empirical map
of their limits. Demonstrating benefit in actual Formula 1 operations requires additional
evidence beyond a simulator. Neither this dossier nor a successful hardware run establishes
that benefit.
The design changes materially. Small one-hot formulations are treated as compiler and
sampling tests, not evidence of computational difficulty. Historical timing data becomes an
optional, permission-gated validation track. Local experiments carry the statistical workload.
Physical hardware has a narrowly defined validation role within the reported 540-second
balance.
Absolute novelty, future quantum advantage, publication acceptance and recruitment by a
team cannot be guaranteed. The study is designed to earn a specific, bounded contribution
through comparisons and reproducible evidence, including evidence that quantum execution is
presently unnecessary.
What remains non-negotiable
AI must perform an evaluated runtime function. The quantum component must be physically
tested if the preceding scientific and budget gates pass. The decision problem must retain
meaningful motorsport mechanisms. No study-specific paid services are assumed. The
completed earlier study and its evidence remain unchanged.
This is independent research. No association with, endorsement by, or access to a Formula 1
team is represented.

3 Related work and novelty boundaries
The literature check establishes nearby precedents, not an exhaustive proof of originality.
Search again before protocol freeze and manuscript submission, recording queries, dates,
versions and full texts actually inspected.
Race strategy optimisation. Carrasco Heine and Thraves develop deterministic and
stochastic dynamic programming for pit strategies [1]. Their model is a baseline source, not
proof that every traffic-coupled team problem is solved. The accessible implementation
provides a route to validating a restricted DP reference [2].
Direct F1 QUBO precedent. Sophie Kolstee's 2026 Leiden bachelor's thesis, previously
unverified in version 2, is available [3]. It studies QUBO formulations using simulated and
quantum-inspired annealing on synthetic instances. It does not report physical QPU
experiments. A first-F1-QUBO claim is unavailable.
Learned racing decisions. Thomas and colleagues study reinforcement-learning pit strategy
with a proprietary Mercedes simulator [4]. Fieni and colleagues study learned multi-agent
racing strategies [5]. Their existence rules out a generic first-AI-strategist claim; their
inaccessible components must not become a hidden dependency.
The nearest systems precedent. Santillana's Pitwall preprint describes calibrated Monte
Carlo strategy evaluation, common random numbers, verified briefings and author-reported
live deployments [6]. Those deployment claims are attributed to the preprint, not
independently verified here. A generic grounded, deadline-aware strategy pipeline is not new.
Quantum methods. QAOA, alternating-operator constructions and warm starts are
established [7-9]. Graph-based and cross-problem parameter transfer also precede this
proposal [10-12]. Gupta and Hassan explicitly develop guarded XY moves and collective
exchanges for rostering [13]. Adapting such moves to pit decisions is not inventing the general
technique.
Simulation and decision theory. Learned algorithm selection is established [27]; it is not
made novel by renaming it an allocator. Common random numbers and metareasoning under
limited resources have prior theory [14-15]. Galbraith's information-processing perspective
may motivate organisational relevance [16], but “late computation has no value” is not a new
general theorem: late results can still support learning or later decisions.
Candidate contribution
Test a margin-aware allocation policy that links learned quantum sampling, uncertainty in the
decision model and the complete decision deadline. Publish the cases in which it declines
quantum execution, as well as those in which it accepts it. Its originality must be assessed
against algorithm selection, metareasoning, racing strategy and quantum benchmarking
together.
The contribution is provisional until the difference from these methods is implemented, tested
and shown to matter.

4 Questions and proposed contributions
Primary question. Does the proposed allocation policy improve the quality of a
deadline-bounded team decision over the strongest included classical policy selected on tuning
data, under the same information and end-to-end resource accounting?
Supporting questions. Does learned parameter selection outperform fixed and
nearest-neighbour transfer? Does a structured circuit yield more useful feasible candidates per
unit of execution cost? How much of any apparent optimisation improvement survives
independent race simulation? Which delays, errors or modelling assumptions prevent useful
quantum participation?
Three contributions to attempt
A decision benchmark. Release generated scenario definitions, causal checkpoint states,
exact small-instance references, solver interfaces and an auditable deadline protocol. A
benchmark is valuable only if it tests meaningful failure modes and can be reused; “open
source” alone is not novelty.
A bounded allocation method. Combine an incumbent, a resolvable improvement margin, a
learned estimate of candidate success, and a measured or explicitly modelled latency budget.
Dispatch only when there is credible room for improvement. Evaluate against always-classical,
always-quantum and simple threshold allocation.
A hardware-linked boundary study. Measure how ideal, noisy and physical circuits change
useful-candidate yield, then identify conditional latency and sample-quality requirements.
Report these as requirements within a defined benchmark, not universal specifications for a
future F1 computer.
Proposed hypotheses to freeze
H1 estimates the paired difference in simulated team loss at the nominal 30-second deadline,
shortened where commitment cutoffs require. H2 compares learned and simple parameter
policies on optimal-sample probability and regret. H3 compares C1 with C0 on raw feasibility
and optimal-sample probability. H2 and H3 are exploratory mechanism analyses; hardware is
descriptive validation. The H1 superiority test proceeds only if the pre-freeze headroom gate
permits it.
The planned minimum worthwhile H1 effect is 0.02 on the normalised team-rank loss defined in
section 9. This is a research threshold, not an elicited team preference. Show the
corresponding unnormalised positions and sensitivity at 0.01 and 0.05. Freeze the threshold
before test outcomes are viewed.
Failure remains informative but not automatically publishable
If AI adds no measurable value, remove the AI-performance claim. If all decision instances have
zero optimisation headroom, report that fact and restrict quantum claims to sampling and
integration. If no substantive difference from prior work remains, redesign before spending
hardware time. A negative result needs a meaningful question and reliable measurement.

5 Decision domain and scope
The core domain is a dry-weather, two-car team choosing whether to pit now, delay by one or
two completed laps, or continue with a frozen feasible continuation policy after a safety-car or
virtual-safety-car checkpoint. The choice includes a tyre compound and an available set. The
simulator carries the consequences to race end.
Use a synthetic 20-car field for the core benchmark. This is a declared model setting, not a
claim about the grid size or rules of every Formula 1 season. Parameterise field size, points
schedules and sporting rules rather than hard-coding “19 rivals.” Any historical extension pins
the applicable season and event documents [17].
State available at the checkpoint
Record lap, race-clock position and each car's pit-entry commitment cutoff; safety regime and
information known about its duration; positions and gaps; compound and tyre age; fuel
estimate and uncertainty; tyre-set inventory; pit-lane status; team service state; completed
compound obligations; and the remaining horizon.
Every field carries a source, unit, availability time and known, inferred or assumed status.
Decisions use the information available at the checkpoint. They do not see the sampled future
safety-car duration, a rival's eventual pit lap or the current lap's final time.
Coupling that is worth modelling
Two team cars may interfere through double stacking, rejoin traffic, service order and position
trade-offs. Rivals can react under a specified policy. Fuel, tyre wear and traffic affect the
continuation. These mechanisms create decision relevance; merely adding binary variables
does not.
Double stacking is normally a delay mechanism, not a universal prohibition. Model
the shared crew and the second car's waiting time. A “never double stack” rule is an optional
declared team policy, not an FIA requirement. Do not invent such a prohibition to create a
difficult constraint graph.
Initial exclusions
Wet transitions, red flags, sprint-specific rules, tyre damage and detailed energy deployment
are excluded from the primary experiment. A checkpoint requiring an unsupported mechanism
is rejected with a recorded reason. Later stress tests may add one mechanism at a time, with
separate results.
The fallback is a feasible team plan revalidated at commitment, including a legal continuation.
“Stay out” is an explicit candidate when feasible, not an unconditional substitute for
compliance with the remaining race rules.

6 Data provenance and real-world validation
Core data are generated. Use independently specified, fictional drivers, teams and track
archetypes. Publish the generator and its distributions. Do not present synthetic checkpoints as
historical races or evidence of deployment.
Formula 1's published guidelines restrict AI use of its protected rights without an express
licence [18]. These are the rights holder's stated conditions, not a determination that every
historical fact or independent dataset is legally unavailable. Document the lawful basis and
applicable source terms for the intended use. FastF1's MIT software licence does not itself
license underlying timing records [19].
Accordingly, no protected timing feed is required for the core study. Before any historical-data
track, record the source's terms and the permission covering the intended analysis, learning
and publication. A software licence or a downloadable file alone is insufficient. No paid licence
is assumed.
Open software and input files are separate
The TUM race-simulation repository is a candidate implementation base under LGPL-3.0 [20].
Audit bundled configurations and data separately before reuse. Preserve applicable notices and
modifications. If a required input lacks clear provenance, replace it with a documented
generated input; do not silently treat it as open data.
Optional historical validation
Proceed only after suitable permission or a clearly applicable data licence is documented. A
candidate chronological split is 2018-2021 for training, 2022 for tuning, 2023 for calibration
and 2024-2025 for testing, subject to actual lawful coverage. This split is proposed, not an
existing corpus.
Aim for at least 30 eligible held-out races across at least 10 circuits and both SC and VSC
conditions. A census must establish whether that coverage exists. Additional checkpoints
within the same race do not substitute for independent races. If coverage is smaller, narrow the
claim and calculate precision from the realised design.
Reconstructed historical availability is not live receipt-time evidence. Finalised lap archives can
leak later information. Tyre-set inventory and team intentions may be unavailable and must
remain explicit assumptions.
The route to actual F1 value
Synthetic evidence can establish an algorithm's behaviour and expose integration
requirements. Historical validation can test plausibility and calibration. A team's prospective
shadow trial would be stronger evidence of operational value. These are distinct levels; only
the level actually completed may appear in the article's claims.
This dossier authorises no contact with a team or data owner. The method and evidence
package can be prepared before any later outreach is requested.

8 Simulator and counterfactual validity
A changed pit decision changes the continuation. Static historical replay cannot reveal the
unobserved alternative. The outcome in this study is conditional on a simulator, its policies and
its uncertainty assumptions.
The preferred starting point is a pinned adapter around TUM's full race simulator, subject to
compatibility and checkpoint-resume tests [20]. Its basic free-track variant is useful for
analytical checks but cannot validate traffic interactions. If the full simulator cannot support the
required checkpoint semantics, implement a restricted independent model and narrow the
claims; do not imply that repository reuse supplies missing validation.
Required mechanism checks
Check tyre-age and fuel updates, event ordering, pit entry and exit, shared-crew service, rejoin
ordering, SC bunching, VSC pace control without equivalent bunching, restart transitions,
compound obligations and end-of-race classification. An SC represented only by multiplying
every lap time is insufficient for the primary SC claim.
Resume a saved checkpoint and compare its continuation with an uninterrupted run under
identical exogenous draws. Verify conservation of race-clock ordering, nonnegative service and
travel times, legal tyre transitions and a consistent treatment of retirement. Compare simple
no-traffic cases against an independently coded analytical or DP model.
Avoid a self-confirming evaluator
The objective compiler and final evaluator must not be the same function under different
names. Build coefficients from a restricted model; score decisions with additional traffic and
stochastic mechanisms. Validate both independently in cases with known answers.
The final evaluator still shares assumptions with the generator. Address this by predeclared
sensitivity models: changed degradation curvature, service variability, restart compression and
rival responsiveness. Agreement between related models is robustness evidence, not empirical
validation of real races.
Rival response
The core rival policy is frozen before test. It may recompute a feasible remaining strategy from
its own observable state. Use a simpler non-reactive policy and a more responsive policy as
sensitivity analyses. Do not feed actual future rival actions into an online comparison.
Monte Carlo confidence intervals describe sampling variation conditional on these choices.
Model discrepancy is addressed by calibration where lawful observations exist, cross-model
checks and sensitivity analysis, and remains a limitation where it cannot be measured.

9 Objective and QUBO specification
For car c, let A(c) be its legal action plans. Each plan contains an immediate pit choice or
continuation plus a specified downstream policy. Binary x(c,a) selects plan a, with exactly one
plan selected per car.
Proxy objective. Minimise the sum of per-plan costs and pairwise interaction costs. Per-plan
cost covers predicted remaining time under the compiler model. Pair terms represent
additional shared-crew and traffic effects. Do not add a separate degradation or traffic penalty
if it is already included in predicted time.
f(x) = Σ a(c,a)x(c,a) + Σ b(a,b)x(1,a)x(2,b).
Before setting penalties, subtract each car's minimum unary cost from its unary coefficients
and retain the offset as a constant. One-hot legality makes this exact on legal plans. It prevents
a large shared race-time baseline from inflating penalties. Recompute bounds after centring
and restore constants for physical-unit reporting.
Any risk term must have explicit units and a linear or quadratic representation, or a separately
verified reduction with auxiliary variables. Do not assume an arbitrary tail-risk functional is a
QUBO. Choose weights on tuning data and show sensitivity including zero. Hard legality and
risk preference are distinct.
QUBO. Add one-hot penalties M[Σ x(c,a) - 1]² and any genuine additional hard-constraint
penalties. Remove individually illegal actions before encoding. Document upper-triangular
coefficient conventions, constants and the mapping x = (1 - Z)/2; verify energies and decoding
exhaustively on small cases.
Let B be the sum of absolute unpenalised coefficients, excluding the constant. Their objective
range over all bit strings is at most B. Require P = 0 on every feasible state, P ≥ v_min > 0
otherwise, and at least one feasible state. Then M > B/v_min suffices for f + MP: an infeasible
penalty exceeds any possible unpenalised gain. Choose a strictly positive numerical margin
and verify the encoded constraints. Version 3.0 used a looser bound but omitted the
feasible-zero condition.
Downstream outcome
For field size F and the two cars' classified ranks r1 and r2, define normalised team-rank loss L
= (r1 + r2 - 2) / [2(F - 1)]. Lower is better. Retirements follow a frozen classification rule. Report
expected loss, individual ranks, team points under the chosen schedule, and a downside
measure separately.
The primary comparison is classical-policy loss minus proposed-policy loss, so positive values
favour the proposed policy. This endpoint measures the modelled team objective. It is not
automatically equivalent to championship utility or a team's actual preferences.
A smaller f does not guarantee smaller expected L. Report ranking disagreement and decision
regret between the proxy and the independent evaluator. This distinction is central to
interpreting any solver result.

10 Exact references and honest scaling
With K actions for one car and one-hot selection, there are K legal plans. With two cars, there
are at most K1 × K2 pairs. Six actions per car use 12 qubits but produce only 36 legal pairs.
Enumerating millions of invalid bit strings would manufacture a classical disadvantage.
If the compiler computes a complete table of every pair's objective, its minimum can be
obtained by a cheap scan of that table. In that case quantum execution tests encoding and
sampling, not a need for optimisation. Include the table construction and minimum search in
the classical comparison.
Three distinct references
Exact proxy optimum. Enumerate all legal choices in the small action set. This certifies the
minimum of f, not the best real race decision.
Finite-simulation reference. Evaluate every legal pair using a fixed offline Monte Carlo bank
on a designated subset. The best estimated action is an offline reference with sampling
uncertainty, not an omniscient true optimum. Use an independent bank to estimate the
selected reference's performance.
Large-instance bound. For extensions, use a free integer solver with valid bounds and
recorded termination gaps. A DP reference is exact only for the assumptions its state and
transition structure represent [1-2].
Implement a linearised binary model independently of the QUBO builder. For a product z = xy,
enforce z ≤ x, z ≤ y and z ≥ x + y - 1. If an integer solver requires coefficient scaling, quantify
rounding error and ties. Check both formulations against enumeration before using the solver
as a reference.
Conditional scaling extension
Treat a multi-stop horizon with team interactions as a separate model extension. It needs its
own variables, transition constraints, QUBO reduction, bounds and frozen protocol after the
small model passes validation. Publish the mapping from every extra variable to a decision.
Keep problem semantics matched across solvers; report preprocessing, memory and wall time.
A conditional stress panel targets 1,200 instances across horizon, action count and interaction
density; it is not authorised by the current single-choice formulation alone. It is classical-first.
Small feasible members can also be simulated quantum mechanically; larger members are
resource estimates or classical benchmarks unless actually executed.
No crossover is guaranteed. If classical methods solve every meaningful instance well within
the deadline, the correct result is no observed optimisation headroom. A tiny hardware
experiment cannot establish how a larger quantum algorithm will scale. Check this on
development cases before launching a large campaign: do not power a superiority test for an
improvement that the formulation makes impossible. Before the full test campaign, either
freeze a meaningful larger model or record H1 as descriptive boundary evidence and omit its
superiority test. If no nontrivial scientific question survives, stop the campaign and redesign.

11 The AI component
The runtime learned component selects a QAOA donor from an offline angle bank. A donor
ranker avoids averaging incompatible periodic angle vectors. It is distinct from the optional
learned dispatch-benefit model in section 14; both train only on training blocks.
Features include action count, coefficient statistics, constraint structure, circuit family, depth
and a cheap incumbent. Exact optima and final evaluation outcomes are offline labels, never
hidden runtime inputs. A feature requiring a solver must include that computation in online
latency.
Scale and fit budget
For each circuit family, use a deterministic coefficient scale s_Q = max(1, the largest absolute
nonconstant encoded coefficient), in fixed coefficient units, and optimise H/s_Q. Record
penalties and s_Q, and use the same transformation at training and inference. Cost angles refer
to the scaled Hamiltonian. Undo scaling before comparing physical decision margins. Angle
banks remain separate by family and depth.
Use 24 training anchors, C0 and C1, p = 1 and p = 2, three seeded starts and at most 80
optimiser evaluations per start: at most 23,040 expectation evaluations. C2 adds a separately
recorded budget only if its conditional local study is admitted.
Select at most eight donors per family-depth pair on training data. Score them on 120 further
training checkpoints and 80 checkpoints from the tuning blocks. Calibration blocks remain
untouched until all fitting and tuning choices are fixed. Save bank construction, fit logs, seeds,
failed fits and CPU time.
Fit a small inspectable model. Fix optimiser termination and compute budgets; no unrecorded
restart replaces a failed fit. Impose angle periodicity only when proven for the implemented
gates and weighted Hamiltonian: arbitrary real-weight cost angles need not have period 2π.
Comparisons and interpretation
Compare learned selection with one fixed donor, nearest-neighbour transfer, random donor
selection and a per-instance best-found variational reference under a fixed offline fit budget.
The reference is not a certified quantum optimum [10-12].
Report optimal-sample probability, raw feasibility, best-of-pool regret and inference time.
Define regret as (f(a) - f*) / (f_max - f*) on legal actions. If the legal range is zero, every feasible
sample has zero regret; an all-infeasible pool still has regret one. Do not use an approximation
ratio that becomes unstable under objective shifts.
The proposed non-inferiority margin against the best-found reference is 0.02 in regret. Report
an upper one-sided 95% paired block interval for learned-minus-reference regret; crossing 0.02
fails that criterion. This comparison is exploratory in this study. Benefit over fixed and
nearest-neighbour transfer needs its own evidence. A null result removes the claimed AI
benefit.

13 What an advanced circuit must justify
The optional advanced construction is a pit-constraint adaptation of guarded exchange
methods, with explicit attribution to prior guarded XY and collective-exchange work [13]. Its
research question is whether the extra controls improve useful yield enough to offset
compilation and noise.
For two actions i and j within a car's one-hot block, use an exchange only when the unchanged
selections of the other car are compatible with both endpoints. The guard must be independent
of the occupation being exchanged, so it commutes with that exchange. Verify the
implemented unitary against the intended transition matrix, including ancilla uncomputation.
Why connectivity matters
Consider two cars choosing slots A or B, with a declared policy requiring different slots. The
legal assignments (A,B) and (B,A) cannot be connected by changing only one car without
entering a forbidden state. A paired exchange can bridge them.
This example explains the mechanism; it does not establish that ordinary double stacking is
illegal. In the primary physical model, stacking usually has a finite cost. If the real model has no
corresponding joint hard constraint, omit the guards instead of manufacturing one.
Enumerate the feasible graph, find connected components under single exchanges, and add
only policy-justified paired moves needed to restore reachability. Publish their action semantics
and gate decompositions. Do not describe a generic graph bridge as a newly invented quantum
primitive.
Admission criteria for C2
The compiler must represent a genuine constraint; ideal circuit checks must show zero
infeasible amplitude to numerical tolerance; the feasible graph must have the intended
connectivity; and the transpiled circuit must fit the registered local resource bounds.
Development results must then show a useful trade-off against C1 at matched fitting and
sampling budgets. Candidate measures are useful feasible probability, best-of-pool loss, native
entangling gates and full execution time. A raw feasibility increase alone is insufficient if useful
candidates become rarer.
If C2 fails, retain the failure report and close that local substudy. C0 and C1 remain the
hardware families. A well-supported conclusion that simpler circuits are preferable is stronger
than a circuit made elaborate to satisfy a novelty label.
Any future claim of a new circuit method requires an additional proof or demonstrably distinct
construction and a renewed prior-art comparison. This dossier does not make that claim.

14 Margin-aware allocation and acceptance
The allocator starts from a legal state and feasible incumbent. Exact enumeration is mandatory
when cheap. If that incumbent attains the exact proxy minimum, record zero proxy headroom
and skip further proxy optimisation. Downstream reranking remains a separate task with
classical comparators.
A reproducible proposed rule
Define z as the observable state features, current incumbent, remaining effective time and
available resource budget. Freeze the generator set and budget grid on tuning blocks. Include
a stop action, classical search and the nominated quantum generator. Reject infeasible or
over-budget options first.
Using training blocks, fit g(z,m,b), the expected reduction in normalised team loss from
generator m with budget b under the complete acceptance-and-fallback policy. Labels use
independent simulation banks and modelled latency regimes; they include failed and late
outputs. Do not fit this model on tuning or calibration blocks.
After tuning, calibration blocks estimate overprediction. For each block take the maximum
positive residual g minus its independently estimated policy benefit over the registered
checkpoints and options. Let q be the empirical 95th percentile of these block residuals,
conservatively allowing for their Monte Carlo error. The score is g - q - λu, where u is estimated
QPU seconds. The primary λ is zero; fixed positive values are sensitivity analyses.
Dispatch the highest positive score that fits the time and usage budgets; otherwise stop.
Resolve ties in favour of the classical option with lower cost. All inference, feature and
incumbent computation consumes decision time. Compare this rule with a fixed threshold and
always-classical allocation.
This is an empirical prediction score, not a confidence bound or a proof of conditional
improvement. With 24 calibration blocks its tail estimate is uncertain. Report held-out
overprediction, harm and coverage; keep shift-panel results separate.
Candidate acceptance
Accept only a timely, current-state-valid candidate that clears the frozen scoring margin under
a matched online scoring budget. Check its pit-entry cutoff. Otherwise use the fallback. Do not
consult final-score estimates or hidden future draws. Evaluation replays this frozen validation
policy causally.
For the separate mathematical bound, let J and f estimate the same outcome in the same units.
If |J(a) - f(a)| ≤ ε uniformly over all relevant actions, then J(a) - J(b) ≤ f(a) - f(b) + 2ε. A
time-error bound cannot certify rank improvement, and an empirical residual quantile is not a
uniform bound.
The allocator must demonstrate value beyond established algorithm selection [27]. Hardware
observations remain too few to fit a general cloud-queue model; scenarios must be labelled as
assumptions.

Shots, policy seeds and simulation worlds are not independent race scenarios. The main
synthetic estimand is the equal-weight mean over the eight declared families, their parameter

Primary local analysis

average paired classical-minus-proposed loss differences across checkpoints. Give blocks equal
weight in the balanced design. Report the mean, a 95% interval, per-block effects and the

comparisons as complete records. This primary procedure estimates uncertainty for the
realised finite-simulation experiment. Do not automatically add inner world resampling to an

19 Monte Carlo design and stopping
Use independent random banks for training labels, online decision scoring and final evaluation.
Final-bank seeds, future draws and outcome scores are hidden from fitting and online scoring.
During evaluation, frozen policies may observe only events revealed at their simulated time,
including causal validity checks at commitment. Selecting a plan using its final-evaluation score
would introduce bias.
Common random numbers
Couple comparable exogenous quantities by keys such as block, replication, driver, lap and
event type. Assign potential draws even when an action changes whether an event occurs.
Merely resetting a global seed is insufficient when different actions consume different numbers
of random draws.
Keep action-dependent causal mechanisms in the simulator; coupling does not mean forcing
different actions to produce identical traffic or service outcomes. Report the observed
covariance of paired losses and compare the variance of differences with independent-draw
controls on development cases [14].
Replication counts
On calibration blocks start with 2,048 evaluation worlds per checkpoint. Select a fixed
production count from 2,048, 8,192 or 32,768 using paired variance. Target a 95% Monte Carlo
half-width no larger than 0.02 at at least 90% of calibration checkpoints. Independently
diagnose Monte Carlo error in the primary block-mean effect; the proposed target is half-width
no larger than 0.002, one fifth of the primary precision target. Account for ten policy seeds and
shared worlds.
If that target cannot be met within resources, report the achieved precision and revise the
endpoint or scope before freezing. Do not silently keep simulation running until a preferred sign
emerges. Within the test run, use the frozen count and report unresolved comparisons.
For a difference bounded in [-1,1], the worst-case normal-approximation half-width at 8,192
independent worlds is about 0.0217; at 2,048 it is about 0.0433. Actual paired variance may be
much smaller. These calculations do not include model error and are not exact finite-sample
coverage guarantees.
Use the same final worlds across arms and policy seeds for a checkpoint and evaluate only
distinct selected plans when possible. Cache continuations by state, effective commitment
time, plan, policies, simulator configuration and random-bank hash. This avoids spending
computation repeatedly on identical decisions.
Two uncertainty levels
Show within-checkpoint Monte Carlo error and across-block uncertainty separately. The
primary block analysis retains finite-simulation variation. Diagnose that component separately
using paired-world variance, respecting shared streams. If it is material, report the
finite-simulation estimand or use a pre-frozen variance-components method; do not append
naive nested resampling. No interval removes structural simulator bias.

22 Conditional readiness analysis
Define pτ as the probability that one raw sample is feasible and beats its stated incumbent by
a frozen proxy margin τ. Component tests use the frozen greedy incumbent; operational tests
use the actual classical incumbent. Also report exact-optimum probability within a pre-frozen
numerical tolerance. Proxy improvement is not necessarily race-outcome improvement.
Under an independent identical-shot model, the probability of at least one such sample in S
shots is 1 - (1 - pτ)^S. To reach success probability at least 1 - α, the required sample count is
the ceiling of log(α) / log(1 - pτ), for 0 < pτ < 1.
For example, pτ = 0.01 needs 299 shots for 95% success, and pτ = 0.001 needs 2,995. These
are conditional calculations. For an estimated pτ, propagate a lower interval bound into the
requirement. Zero observed successes do not prove pτ = 0; a zero lower bound supplies no
finite certified shot requirement. Only an exact objective argument establishes impossible
improvement. Drift can invalidate the independent-shot model.
A full boundary
The quantum path must fit state processing, encoding, compilation, queueing, execution,
retrieval and validation inside the decision deadline, while providing enough useful probability
to justify replacing the incumbent.
Report a surface over queue delay, circuit duration, shot count and useful yield. Mark
measured points separately from noise simulations and hypothetical scenarios. A single
average fidelity is insufficient: error location, connectivity, parameter quality and the feasible
subspace also matter.
If the incumbent already attains the exact objective optimum, pτ is zero for any strictly positive
improvement margin. No finite shot count creates missing headroom. This is an important
boundary, not a failed plotting case to remove.
Future hardware claims
A scenario with a shorter queue or fewer gate errors shows what would be required under its
assumptions. It does not prove that a provider will deliver those conditions, that the algorithm
retains its quality at larger size, or that classical methods will remain unchanged.
Do not translate a small NISQ noise sweep into fault-tolerant logical-qubit requirements without
a separate algorithm, error-correction and resource-estimation analysis. Do not fit an
exponential quantum speedup from small problem sizes.
Practical output
Produce a rerunnable table stating, for each instance family, the strongest classical result,
available headroom, acceptable model error, useful-sample requirement and full latency
allowance. A team can use this to reject unsuitable integrations or identify a narrow future
shadow-test condition. That is a concrete decision-support output even where quantum is not
selected.

26 Gates and useful outcomes
Gate A, provenance. Every required software and data dependency has a recorded licence or
permission. Generated inputs remain the default. Failure blocks the affected input, not
unrelated local work.
Gate B, decision validity. The checkpoint, continuation, SC/VSC behaviour and team
interaction mechanisms pass independent checks. Failure narrows the domain or requires
repair before comparisons.
Gate C, formulation. Direct costs, QUBO energies and independent classical references
agree on small cases. The legal action count and compiler cost are reported. Failure blocks
quantum experiments.
Gate D, learning and circuits. Models respect separate training, tuning and calibration
blocks. Core C0/C1 circuits pass preparation, transition and compilation checks. C2 is
separately gated. Held-out claims must use the frozen comparisons.
Gate E, scientific value. Before the full test campaign, record whether exact classical
solution removes all proxy headroom. If so, omit an impossible superiority test. Proceed only
with a separately justified boundary or mechanism question, or validate a larger model before
freeze. If no substantive contribution survives comparison with prior work, redesign before QPU
use.
Gate F, precision and budget. The chosen experiment count follows development-only
variance and resource evidence. Hardware reservations fit verified free access with the reserve
intact. Failure leads to a smaller explicitly limited study or a later free allocation, not
unapproved spending.
Outcomes that would help a strategy group
A validated classical-first fallback and causal checkpoint interface can improve reproducibility
of strategy research. A measured map of proxy error can show where better modelling matters
more than faster optimisation. A candidate-yield and latency boundary can prevent premature
quantum integration. A successful learned circuit policy could justify a carefully scoped
follow-up.
These are proposed benefits. Demonstrated lap-time, points or operational gains for a real
team require appropriate real-world evaluation. A synthetic benchmark must not be marketed
as evidence that an actual race result would have improved.
Career evidence
Prepare a concise technical portfolio after results: an executable benchmark, a transparent
hardware record, a reproducible negative-and-positive findings report, and a short explanation
of the integration conditions. Explain the author's scientific decisions and ability to audit
AI-generated code.
This can provide credible evidence of skill across AI, quantum methods and motorsport
decision-making. It cannot make the author a guaranteed recruitment target. The strongest
positioning comes from solving and explaining a specific problem well.

27 Publication and claim discipline
The later article should follow the completed evidence: motivation, related work, causal
decision model, algorithm and circuit definitions, protocol, results, robustness, limitations and
reproducibility. Journal selection follows the demonstrated contribution, available validation
and verified fee policy.
Do not assume a particular journal accepts synthetic-only evidence, a negative hardware study
or a DBA framing. Do not assume an article-processing charge will be waived. The zero
additional spending requirement applies to publication choices too.
Allowed only with matching evidence
“The learned donor selector improved useful probability on the held-out generated panel”
requires that exact comparison and interval. “The structured circuit reduced invalid raw
samples on IBM” requires physical paired evidence. “The allocator improved simulated team
outcomes” requires the independent evaluator and full deadline accounting.
“The system improved Formula 1 performance,” “quantum advantage,” “first quantum F1
optimiser,” “guaranteed future superiority,” “real-time team deployment,” and “a new guarded
mixer” are not supported by this plan.
Important remaining limitations
Synthetic parameter blocks are not actual races. Historical counterfactuals remain
model-based even with lawful data. Model assumptions can favour a solver. Small circuits may
not represent large problems. The physical sample is small and backend-specific. Queue
conditions and provider policies change. A locally measured CPU budget is not a team's
production environment.
General novelty cannot be established by a finite literature search. A claim of originality must
identify the precise algorithmic or empirical difference from the closest work and show why
that difference is useful.
What would justify proceeding to a manuscript
The implementation must be reproducible; the main scientific claim, whether positive or
negative, must be supported by the planned comparisons; uncertainty must match the actual
sample; and the contribution must remain meaningful after limitations are stated plainly. A
successful pipeline run by itself does not meet those conditions.
If the results reveal no useful quantum role, report the boundary and the reasons. If they reveal
no meaningful novel contribution at all, do not manufacture one with stronger language. Use
the evidence to define the next research question.
The aim is a defensible contribution with a credible path toward real motorsport validation, not
a predetermined positive story about quantum computing.

30 Protocol freeze record
This dossier is a design recommendation. The following fields become concrete during the
implementation and development pilot. No test outcome may be used to choose them.

Completion standard
Before declaring a local campaign complete, reconcile planned versus actual cases and
repetitions, check all evidence files, reproduce tables from the raw records and report missing
units.
Before declaring hardware complete, reconcile every intent, job status and usage entry,
preserve all returned data and account for the reserve. A target count does not override the
budget.
Before drafting the article, confirm that its central claim survives the strongest fair comparator,
has adequate stated precision and is supported by the level of domain validation actually
achieved.

isolation, environment inspection, the run ledger and generated-data schema. No quantum
